## 4. Content Gaps & Corrections

### Source → Corrected

- "75 billion IoT devices by 2025"  
  **Removed:** prediction is outdated and not suitable as current fact.

- "5G is necessary for IoT growth"  
  **Corrected:** 5G enables some IoT classes, but many use cases depend on NB-IoT/LTE-M/Wi-Fi/BLE/LPWA.

- "5G is 100× faster than 4G"  
  **Qualified:** theoretical peaks vs user-experienced speeds.

- "5G reaches 10 Gbps"  
  **Qualified:** peak capability is not expected continuous device throughput.

- "5G has wider range than 4G"  
  **Corrected:** coverage depends on spectrum and deployment.

- "1 million devices/km²"  
  **Qualified:** IMT-2020 minimum evaluation requirement for mMTC scenario.

- "1 ms latency"  
  **Qualified:** user-plane URLLC requirement under defined evaluation conditions, not full application E2E.

- "MIMO sends independent copies so at least one arrives"  
  **Corrected:** MIMO also supports spatial multiplexing and beamforming; diversity is only one mode.

- "NB-IoT is simply a 5G NR technology"  
  **Clarified:** NB-IoT and LTE-M originated in LTE and are part of the cellular massive IoT evolution; RedCap is NR-based.

- "All smart-city IoT benefits from 5G"  
  **Qualified:** connectivity choice depends on bandwidth, power, coverage, latency and cost.

- "5G makes IoT secure"  
  **Corrected:** network security does not replace device/product security.

- "6G is a near-deployment replacement"  
  **Updated:** IMT-2030/6G remains under standardization/study in 2026.

### Weak/unrelated references removed

The source bibliography included citations unrelated to 5G/IoT, including 2024 assistive-vision papers. These were not retained.
